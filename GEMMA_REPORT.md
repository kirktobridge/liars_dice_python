# Gemma vs CPU Archetypes — Empirical Performance Report

_Run: 180 games (30 per matchup × 6 matchups), 2,432 LLM decisions logged, 8h 44m wall-clock on `gemma4:e4b` via Ollama. Generated by [scripts/gemma_analysis.py](scripts/gemma_analysis.py); raw decision-by-decision traces in `scripts/gemma_traces.jsonl` (gitignored)._

## TL;DR

**Gemma cannot play Liar's Dice at this prompt budget.** She lost every game (0/180; 95% Wilson upper bound 2.0%). For reference, a uniformly random play policy at a 5-player table would win ~20% (≈36 games); Gemma is many standard deviations below random.

Three concrete failure modes account for almost all of it:

1. **Severe under-bidding.** 22.5% of her bids are `count=1` — *below the legal minimum of 2*. The engine accepts these and they get insta-challenged. 56% of all bids are `count ≤ 2` regardless of how many dice are on the table.
2. **Reflexive challenging.** Once any bid history exists, ~50% of her decisions are `challenge`, with 1–9% accuracy depending on archetype. She is calling truthful bids as bluffs.
3. **Zero opponent adaptation.** Median aggression is 0.07–0.10 across all five opponent archetypes — Gemma plays the same way against a Reckless Buccaneer table as against a Stoic Quartermaster table.

What does work: JSON output is essentially perfect (1 fallback in 2,432 decisions, and that one was an Ollama 60s timeout, not malformed output). When she does bid, 78% of bids name a face she actually holds (15% on wilds, 7% on faces she doesn't hold), so she reads her dice — she just can't size her bid.

## Methodology

- **Model**: [`gemma4:e4b`](src/constants.py#L1) via Ollama, temperature 1.0 (Google guidance), 60s timeout
- **Sample**: 30 games per matchup × 6 matchups = 180 games, seeds 0..29 in each matchup
- **Table**: 5 players (1 Gemma + 4 CPU archetype copies), 6 dice each at start
- **Trace capture**: every `query_llm` / `query_llm_stream` call intercepted via [`set_llm_debug_listener`](src/llm_client.py#L31), prompt + response + parse outcome appended to `scripts/gemma_traces.jsonl`
- **Wilson 95% CIs** reported on win rates so the 0/30 cells are honest about their upper bound
- **Bug fix mid-run**: smoke testing surfaced a crash when Gemma chose `challenge` on the round-opening turn (no prior bid → `prev_bid.count` on `None`). Added a guard at [src/strategy.py:519-522](src/strategy.py#L519-L522) that treats illegal-on-open moves as parse failures and falls back to `CPUStrategy`. After the fix, zero illegal-on-open events occurred in 2,432 decisions.

Almost half of her actions, in every matchup, are challenges. Drilling into the trace by prompt history length:

| Recent history shown to Gemma | bid | raise | challenge | spot_on |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 entries (round-opening) | **100%** | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| 3–5 entries | 35% | 7% | **56%** | 1% |
| 6+ entries | 39% | 8% | **52%** | 1% |

Once any bidding history is in the prompt, Gemma defaults to challenge in slightly over half of her turns. She is not weighing whether the standing bid is plausible — she's reading "history exists → challenge" as the modal action.

### Bid sizing

- **Count distribution** of all 1,221 bid/raise decisions:

  | Count | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8+ |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | Bids | 275 | 409 | 256 | 95 | 99 | 50 | 15 | 8 |
  | Share | 22.5% | 33.5% | 21.0% | 7.8% | 8.1% | 4.1% | 1.2% | 0.7% |

  Over 56% of bids are ≤ 2 even though tables routinely hold 24–30 dice. Optimal opening counts at 30 dice on the table average 5–7 (one third of dice show any given non-1 face).

- **Count = 1 is below the legal minimum** ([src/constants.py:5](src/constants.py#L5) sets `MINIMUM_BID = 2`). The engine has no validation guard, so the bid stands but is trivially over-claimed: every truthful opponent will challenge and win. This single failure mode appears 275 times — about 11% of all Gemma decisions.

- **Aggression** (claimed count / total dice on table) by matchup, p25 / median / p75:

  | Matchup | p25 | median | p75 |
  |---|---:|---:|---:|
  | vs Salty | 0.07 | 0.07 | 0.11 |
  | vs Reckless | 0.07 | 0.08 | 0.12 |

  | vs Stoic | 0.05 | 0.08 | 0.12 |
  | vs Wild Card | 0.07 | 0.10 | 0.14 |
  | vs Mixed | 0.07 | 0.08 | 0.12 |

  No-information baseline is **0.33** (any non-1 face appears in ~⅓ of dice, including wilds). Gemma's medians cluster at 0.07–0.10 — **3–4× too conservative** in every matchup. The spread between matchups is essentially noise.

### Face selection works

Distribution of bid faces relative to Gemma's own hand (1,220 valid bids):

- **78.0%** name a face Gemma actually holds
- **15.2%** bid on ones (wilds — defensible, since 1s count for any face)
- **6.9%** bid on a face she does *not* hold

Face selection is the one component of her play that's behaving sensibly. Bid sizing is what's broken.

A few patterns are real:

- **Attentive opponents punish her bluffs harder.** The 14 and 15 caught-bluffing counts come from `vs_crafty` and `vs_mixed` (which contains a Crafty Captain). Wild Card opponents — who pay almost no attention — caught her only 3 times in 30 games because they don't update on her behavior.
- **Survival is slightly higher vs Wild Card** (mean finish 1.10) — the only matchup where her bluffs sometimes survive long enough to matter. This is consistent with the caught-bluffing data.

## Per-Matchup Deep Dives

### vs 4× Salty Veteran — 0/30, mean finish 1.00

Salty Veterans are low-risk and very attentive (risk 30, attentiveness 80, bluff 10 per [src/constants.py:24](src/constants.py#L24)) — they almost never bluff and they remember opponents who do. Gemma faced 30 dice on the table, made tiny bids, and got challenged. Caught bluffing 7 times across 30 games.

Most of the elimination action happened in the opening rounds, before any "recent history" had a chance to inform her decisions:

```text
--- PROMPT (vs_salty seed 0, opening turn) ---
Your dice: [4, 4, 1, 3, 5, 4]
Total dice held by other players: 24
Previous action: action=START

--- RESPONSE ---
{"action": "bid", "count": 2, "face": 4}
```

She has **four effective fours** (three 4s + one wild). Expected non-1 fours among 24 opponent dice: ~8. So a defensible opening is `bid 6 fours` or even `8 fours`. She bid `2 fours` — about 4× too low. The Salty Veterans then escalate normally and Gemma is the first to be put under pressure.

### vs 4× Reckless Buccaneer — 0/30, mean finish 1.03

Reckless Buccaneers (risk 80, att 30, bluff 60) bluff often and don't pay attention. In principle this should give Gemma the most room: opponent over-claims should be the one situation where her ~50% challenge rate would actually pay off. It doesn't help — challenge accuracy is 2% here, no different from Salty. She's not reading aggression in opponent bids; she's challenging on autopilot.

Caught bluffing only 5 times because Reckless opponents don't update on her play.

### vs 4× Crafty Captain — 0/30, mean finish 1.07 — *the toughest matchup*

Crafty Captains (risk 50, att **90**, bluff 35) read opponent profiles and adjust their challenge thresholds. They caught Gemma bluffing **14 times in 30 games** — almost twice the rate of any homogeneous archetype lineup (other than Mixed, which contains a Crafty). Yet because Crafty also makes balanced, hard-to-challenge bids of their own, Gemma's challenges land at 8%, only marginally above pure noise.

This is the matchup that most clearly illustrates the asymmetry: Crafty exploits Gemma's predictability while denying her exploitable bids of their own.

### vs 4× Stoic Quartermaster — 0/30, mean finish 1.03

Stoic Quartermasters (risk 15, att 70, bluff 5) are the tightest, most pessimistic openers. The clearest example of Gemma's underbidding came in this matchup at seed 11:

```text
--- PROMPT (vs_stoic seed 11) ---
Your dice: [5, 1, 5, 1, 1]
Total dice held by other players: 24
Recent history:
  - Stoic Quartermaster 1 bid 6 twos
  - Stoic Quartermaster 2 raised to 7 twos
  - Stoic Quartermaster 3 bid 7 fives
  - Stoic Quartermaster 4 bid 7 sixes
Previous action: action=START

--- RESPONSE ---
{"action": "bid", "count": 2, "face": 5}
```

Gemma holds **five effective fives** (two 5s plus three wilds) out of her own 5 dice — every single die in her hand counts toward fives. With 24 opponent dice, the *expected* number of fives is 8. A defensible bid is somewhere in `8–10 fives`. She bid `2 fives` on a hand that already had 5 of them. This is the clearest single-trace evidence that Gemma is not multiplying through.

(One note: this trace appears at the start of a fresh round, not the start of the game — the "Previous action: action=START" line is reused on every round opener. The "Recent history" entries are from the previous round.)

### vs 4× Wild Card — 0/30, mean finish 1.10 — *the longest survival*

Wild Cards (risk 70, att 50, bluff 80) bluff aggressively and don't track opponents. Gemma's bluffs land more often here (only 3 caught in 30 games) and her challenges are slightly more accurate (9% vs ~2%) because Wild Cards over-claim the most. This is the one matchup where Gemma's pathologies are partially camouflaged by an even noisier opponent. She still loses every game, but rounds last a hair longer.

### vs 1 of each archetype — 0/30, mean finish 1.00

The mixed table contains one Crafty Captain, and Crafty alone is enough to drive caught-bluffing back up to **15** — the highest of any matchup. The Crafty player is reading Gemma's pattern (low bids, frequent over-claim relative to win probability) and selectively challenging. The other three archetypes contribute noise.

This matchup also produced the only fallback in the entire 2,432-decision run:

```text
--- PROMPT (vs_mixed seed 19, response-to-bid) ---
Your dice: [5, 4]
Total dice held by other players: 24
Recent history:
  - Crafty raised to 6 threes
  - Stoic bid 6 fours
  - … (10 history entries) …
Previous action: action=RAISE, bid=Bid(count=5, face=6)

--- RESPONSE (FALLBACK, 60.06s) ---
[empty — Ollama 60s read timeout]
```

CPUStrategy's challenge logic took over and the game continued. The 60s timeout is the only one observed; ~99.96% of decisions completed in under 36s.

### Reflexive challenge example

The clearest single-decision evidence of reflexive challenging:

```text
--- PROMPT (vs_mixed seed 29) ---
Your dice: [4, 6, 6, 5, 5]
Recent history:
  - Salty raised to 4 fours
  - … 7 more history entries …
  - Stoic bid 2 fours
Previous action: action=BID, bid=Bid(count=2, face=4)

--- RESPONSE ---
{"action": "challenge", "count": 2, "face": 4}
```

Stoic just bid `2 fours` — the **legal minimum** in 30 dice. The probability that 2 fours (counting wilds) exist among 30 dice is ~99.99%. Gemma challenged it. The action JSON even includes `count` and `face` for a challenge, which suggests she's pattern-matching the format more than the semantics.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

- **No archetype gives Gemma a winnable game**, but Wild Card lets her last marginally longer because Wild Cards don't update on opponent behavior. Mixed (with one Crafty) and Salty are the worst.
- **The 49–51% challenge rate is the dominant pathology.** It's stable across all matchups and prompt-history lengths, which suggests it's a property of the prompt structure or model bias, not a mis-read of any specific game state.
- **Bid count = 1 is the second pathology.** 275 of 1,221 bids (22.5%) are below the legal minimum. The engine accepts them; opponents trivially win. The prompt does not currently state the minimum bid; even adding `count must be >= 2` would likely eliminate this failure mode.
- **JSON formatting is essentially perfect.** 1 fallback in 2,432 decisions, and that fallback was an HTTP timeout, not a malformed response. Whatever is wrong with Gemma's play is *strategic*, not *output-format*.
- **Latency**: median 12.9s, p95 32.5s, single 60s timeout. CPU-bound (this run was on Ollama with no GPU). For a real-time UX, every Gemma turn would feel like a long pause.

## Why is Gemma losing?

Combining the failure modes:

1. **She bids tiny counts** → opponents trivially raise above her, and on a round where her bid is the terminal action, the next opponent's challenge wins automatically (count=1 is always under-claim in 30 dice).
2. **She challenges roughly 50% of the time after any history exists** → most challenged bids are legitimate, and she loses a die per failed challenge.
3. **She doesn't adapt to opponent type** → her play vs. a Stoic table (almost no bluffing) is identical to her play vs. a Wild Card table (constant bluffing). The "recent history" portion of the prompt is contributing pattern triggers (`history exists → challenge`) rather than informing strategy.

These three together produce a player who loses dice every round she takes a turn, and who is therefore the first to be eliminated almost every game.

## What this run did *not* test

- **Different prompt structures.** The current prompt at [src/strategy.py:539-565](src/strategy.py#L539-L565) doesn't explain the legal minimum (`count >= 2`), doesn't describe optimal sizing (≈ ⅓ of total dice as a starting point), and presents history as a flat list without per-opponent aggression cues. A prompt update is the cheapest plausible intervention.
- **Different temperature.** Run was at 1.0 (Google guidance for Gemma); the production default is 0.3 ([src/strategy.py:468](src/strategy.py#L468)). A lower temperature might tighten the action mix but probably won't fix the strategic errors.
- **Different model size.** This was `gemma4:e4b` (the e4b efficient variant). A larger Gemma might solve the bid-sizing math.
- **Multi-shot prompting.** The current prompt is single-shot. A few in-context examples of correct play would likely help.

## Limitations

- **Sample size**: 30 games per matchup. With a 0/30 result the upper Wilson bound is 11.4%, which rules out "Gemma is competitive" but cannot resolve fine differences between matchups.
- **Single seed family**: seeds 0..29 share dice rolls across matchups, so opening conditions are matched. This is good for cross-archetype comparison but means the dice variance baked into "is this a winnable seed" is not sampled.
- **Per-bid attribution**: the round-level collector ([src/stats_collector.py](src/stats_collector.py)) only stores the *terminal* bidder and challenger of each round. "Times caught bluffing" counts rounds where Gemma was the terminal bidder and the challenger won — it under-counts rounds Gemma bid and was raised over (rather than challenged). The decision-level trace JSONL is the source of truth for action distribution and bid sizing.
- **Latency variance**: this run was on a CPU-only Ollama on a Windows host (172.19.176.1). Times will differ on a GPU host or a different model release.
- **Model drift**: if `gemma4:e4b` is updated upstream, results will not be reproducible.
