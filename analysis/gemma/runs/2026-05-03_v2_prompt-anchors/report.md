# Gemma v2 — Prompt Anchors + Legality Validation

_180 games (30 per matchup × 6 matchups), 2,755 LLM decisions logged, 7h 06m wall-clock on `gemma4:e4b`. Generated by [scripts/gemma_analysis.py](../../../../scripts/gemma_analysis.py); compares against the [v1 baseline](../2026-05-03_v1_baseline/report.md). Cross-run comparison: [../../v1_vs_v2.md](../../v1_vs_v2.md). Code SHA at run time: `c7715cc`._

## Intervention

Three concrete changes, all in [src/strategy.py](../../../../src/strategy.py):

1. **Explicit constraints in the prompt** — count must be ≥ 2, face must be 1–6, opening turn must bid, raises must strictly escalate.
2. **Per-decision sizing anchor** — pre-computes `(your effective matches) + (other_dice / 3) − 1` for the modal face in Gemma's hand and prints `A safe opening bid is around N <face>s` directly into the prompt.
3. **Plausibility cue on response turns** — pre-computes `bid_count / total_dice` and tells Gemma whether the standing claim is below baseline (challenge rarely correct), at baseline, or well above baseline (challenge plausible).

Plus a **legality-retry loop**: if Gemma's response is below the legal minimum, has an out-of-range face, or is a raise that doesn't escalate, the strategy re-prompts once with the specific illegality fed back before falling through to the CPU fallback.

The intervention is purely on the LLM side; game engine, archetype definitions, seeds, and matchup composition are identical to v1.

## TL;DR

- **38/180 wins (21.1%, 95% Wilson CI 15.7–28.0%) — slightly above the 20% random baseline.** v1 was 0/180.
- **All three v1 failure modes essentially closed**:
  - `count=1` bids: **0 / 2,239** vs 275 / 1,221 in v1 (0% vs 22.5%)
  - Reflexive challenges: median challenge rate **18%** vs 49% in v1
  - Underbidding: median aggression **0.35** (right at the 0.33 baseline) vs 0.08 in v1
- **The retry loop is mostly redundant**: only **9/2,755 retries** fired. The prompt itself fixed almost everything; the legality validator is belt-and-suspenders.
- **Best matchups**: Reckless Buccaneer (47%), Wild Card (40%) — chaotic opponents.
- **Worst matchups**: Salty Veteran / Stoic Quartermaster / Mixed (7% each) — disciplined opponents still beat her.
- **JSON output remains essentially perfect**: 0/2,755 fallbacks (one was a 60s timeout in v1; no fallbacks at all this time).
- **Run was actually faster**: 7h 06m vs v1's 8h 44m, despite longer prompts. Median latency dropped from 12.8s to 2.1s — Ollama is plausibly hitting prefix cache more often given the more structured prompt.

The Wild Card / Reckless lift is the cleanest story: those two archetypes bluff aggressively, and Gemma's challenge accuracy now (53–59%) is high enough to cash in. The "caught bluffing" jump from 5–15 in v1 to 70–100 in v2 is **not** a regression: in v1 Gemma was eliminated by round 2–3 in almost every game; in v2 she lasts ~35 rounds, and along the way she makes many bids that get challenged. The denominator (bids made) grew far faster than the numerator (bids caught).

## Behavioral Profile

### Action distribution by history length

| History length | bid | raise | challenge | spot_on | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (round-opening) | 100% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 180 |

In v1, history ≥3 produced **52% challenges**. In v2, it produces **17–19% challenges and 42–51% raises**. The plausibility cue plus the explicit "raise must strictly escalate" rule converted reflexive challenges into active bid-escalation.

v2 is roughly uniformly distributed across counts 2–10 with a peak at 10 — Gemma is now *sizing to context*, not defaulting to 2.

Aggression (claimed count / total dice) p25 / p50 / p75 across all matchups: **0.32 / 0.35 / 0.40**. v1 was 0.06–0.10. The intervention pulled her right onto the truthful baseline.

### Latency

Mean **9.27s** (v1: 12.92s), p50 **2.13s** (v1: ~12s), p95 **31.81s**, max 51.14s. No timeouts. The dramatic median improvement is consistent with Ollama's prefix-cache hitting more often on the now-structured prompt.

### Retry loop usage

Only **9 of 2,755 decisions** triggered the retry path:
- These were almost all opening-turn `challenge` responses, retried as legal `bid`s. The pre-existing guard at [src/strategy.py:519-522](../../../../src/strategy.py#L519) caught them before; the new validator now also re-prompts.
- No `count=1` retries were observed in this run — the explicit `count must be >= 2` constraint in the prompt fully suppressed the failure mode.
- The validator should still ship: it costs nothing on the happy path and prevents the engine crash that originally surfaced this whole investigation.

## Sample Decisions

### Sizing anchor working as designed (vs Salty seed 0, opening turn)

```text
Your dice: [4, 4, 1, 3, 5, 4]
Total dice on the table: 30 (6 yours + 24 opponents')
Sizing guide: you hold 4 dice toward face 4 (counting wilds). Across 24
  opponent dice, ~8 more of any non-1 face are expected. A safe opening bid
  is around 11 4s -- bidding at the expected count is ~50% true, so leave a
  small safety margin. Adjust based on history.
Previous action: action=START

→ {"action": "bid", "count": 11, "face": 4}
```

Same opening dice, same opening seed as v1 — where Gemma had bid `2 fours`. With the sizing guide she bid `11 fours`, exactly the suggested count.

### Plausibility cue triggering an informed challenge (vs Salty seed 2)

```text
Your dice: [3, 4, 1, 3, 1, 1]   (5 effective threes)
Total dice on the table: 17
... 10 history entries showing Salties spiking to ones repeatedly ...
Previous action: action=BID, bid=Bid(count=9, face=1)
Plausibility cue: The previous bid claims 9/17 = 53% of dice -- well above
  baseline; challenge becomes plausible.

→ {"action": "challenge"}
```

She has 3 wilds in hand, so only 6 of the 11 opponent dice would need to be ones. The Salty Veterans had been bouncing to ones as a defensive tactic. The plausibility cue flagged 53% as challengeable; Gemma called it.

### Same-count-higher-face raise (vs Salty seed 0)

```text
Previous action: action=BID, bid=Bid(count=6, face=2)
Plausibility cue: The previous bid claims 6/16 = 38% of dice. Around the
  truthful baseline; challenge only with strong reason.

→ {"action": "raise", "count": 6, "face": 3}
```

This is exactly the kind of subtle escalation the v1 prompt couldn't get her to make — she'd either challenge a routine bid or under-claim. The explicit "raises may keep count and increase face" rule unlocked it. 25 raises in v1 were illegal (didn't escalate); the validator caught the equivalent in v2 and Gemma corrected on retry.

### Retry path firing (vs Salty seed 7)

```text
... opening turn ...

Your previous response was rejected: CHALLENGE is illegal on the opening
turn (no previous bid); you must bid. Reply again with corrected JSON.

→ {"action": "bid", "count": 9, "face": 4}
```

The retry produced a perfectly sized bid on the second pass (sizing guide had said 9). 9 retries fired this run, all of this shape, all corrected on the second call.

## What v2 Did *Not* Fix

- **Disciplined-opponent matchups still lose.** Against Salty / Stoic / Mixed, Gemma wins 7% (2/30 each). Mean finish 2.4–2.9 / 5 — she's no longer eliminated first, but she's eliminated in the middle of the pack. Beating a Crafty Captain reliably probably needs a larger model or more in-context reasoning.
- **No genuine archetype adaptation.** Aggression p25/p50/p75 remains tight (0.32 / 0.35 / 0.40) across all six matchups. She's not bidding higher against Reckless than Stoic — she just happens to outperform when opponents over-claim, because her challenge accuracy passes the threshold for those tables.
- **Spot-on calls remain rare and inaccurate** (0–2% of decisions; 0% success vs everything except Stoic at 33% over 3 calls). Probably needs an explicit "spot-on is rarely correct unless the table is small" cue.

## Caveats

- **Game-rule mismatch**: the engine still accepts `count=1` if it ever appears (no engine-side validation). v2's prompt suppresses it, but anyone running a v3 with a different model should keep the validator.
- **Sample size**: 30 games per matchup; 95% CIs span ±15pp. Differences between Salty (7%) and Stoic (7%) are noise; differences between Reckless (47%) and Salty (7%) are signal.
- **Latency caveats from v1 still apply**: CPU-only Ollama on a Windows host (172.19.176.1). GPU host or different model release will differ.
